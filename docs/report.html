Proof of concept · synthetic B2B invoices · 6,468 invoices, 2023–2026

Biomix invoice triage

Every incoming invoice is checked before it is paid. Plain rules handle the clear cases, a model called Jev looks at the doubtful ones, and only what is still doubtful reaches a person. On this data, 99.6% of invoices are approved automatically and 21 go to a reviewer.

Tier 1: the rules

Arithmetic, prices, commissions, payment schedules and party data are checked line by line. The synthetic data is clean, so those rules all pass. The two rules about repeated billing do the real work.

RuleWhat it checksResult
line_arithmeticEvery line: package_quantity ≥ 1, unit price > 0, line amount = quantity × unit priceall pass
invoice_totalInvoice total = Σ line amounts; line_count = number of lines; lines numbered 1..nall pass
commissionEach line's commission = 5% rounded half up; invoice commission = Σ line commissionsall pass
price_listUnit price = the product's list price for the year, as seen on earlier invoices; a new year's first price must be +0% … +10% per year above the last list priceall pass
payment_scheduleKnown schedule; installment count and first due date match it; 30 days apart; amounts sum to the total, differ by ≤ 1 cent, leftover cents firstall pass
party_consistencyInvoice seller and business unit = the customer's; every line and installment matches the invoice header; each product exists, belongs to the invoice's business unit and keeps its catalogue name and categoryall pass
duplicate_linesSame products and quantities as an invoice of the same customer ≤ 7 days earlierall pass
rebilled_productsBilled ≤ 2 days after the same customer's previous invoice with only products already on it (a partial rebill; Phase 4 found this rule catches them better than Jev)14 flagged
near_duplicate_windowBilled ≤ 2 days after the same customer's previous invoice (Jev decides)418 to Jev

Tier 2: what Jev was asked

Is this invoice the same order as the customer's previous invoice (billed again in whole or in part, or one order split across the two invoices), rather than a separate new order?

Jev (model jev-1.13.0) sees both invoices and the customer's history and answers yes or no with a probability. Scores ran from 0.05 to 0.46, median 0.19. Anything at or above 0.39 goes to a reviewer.

Does it catch the right invoices?

The data has no answer key, so the evaluation plants cases with a known answer. Each one is built from real invoices: a rebill, a rebill with changed quantities, a split order, or a real new order moved next to the previous invoice. Then every case goes through the same rules and the same Jev question.

Jev spots exact rebills

Against real new orders its AUC is 1.00 on tune and 1.00 on test. Rebills with changed quantities are harder: only 40% / 23% score above the threshold.

A plain rule does better

“Billed ≤ 2 days later with only products already on the previous invoice” catches every planted rebill, both kinds, and flags 1.7% / 1.7% of real new orders. It now runs in tier 1, and Jev stays behind it as a second opinion.

Splits are left alone

Jev rates split orders as less alike than real new orders (AUC 0.23 / 0.18). That is acceptable: a split bills each line once, so it is approved. False alarms on new orders end at 1.7% / 3.3%.

Limits

  • All data is synthetic. Customer, product and invoice numbers are made up.
